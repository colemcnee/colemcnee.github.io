Robert Llewellyn believed the aliens were sending him messages, that the messages were in the Dewey
Decimal numbers on the spines of library books, and that the proofs were music. This is his machine:
two people work it, every call number entered becomes a layer — the class digit picks the
instrument, the decimals pick the job, the author's Cutter number picks the tune — and six numbers
make a piece for six voices. At the end it writes the argument down as an actual proof, and proves
the small thing it can: that this arrangement does not come round again inside a performance.
